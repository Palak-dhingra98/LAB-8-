Workplace Writing II: Summary Fidelity and Report Scaffolding
 
Step 1 — Understand Summary Fidelity
Summary fidelity means accurately representing the original source without changing its meaning, adding unsupported information, removing critical information, or presenting assumptions as facts.
A faithful summary should preserve important facts and figures, distinguish different types of information, and include relevant limitations from the original source.
Important Principle
A shorter summary is useful only when it remains faithful to the original source.
 
Step 2 — Read the Source Passage

During August, an airport customer-service team reviewed 120 passenger comments. Forty-five comments concerned long queues at security screening, 30 concerned unclear directional signs, 20 concerned baggage delays, 15 concerned limited seating near departure gates and 10 praised staff helpfulness.
The report did not measure overall passenger satisfaction. It did not compare the airport with other airports. No financial impact was calculated. Airport management introduced two temporary information desks near the security area for a two-week trial. The effect of this trial had not been measured when the report was prepared.

Step 15 — Student Reflection
1. What does summary fidelity mean?
Summary fidelity means keeping the meaning and important information of the original source accurate when creating a shorter version.
2. What is the difference between an omission and an invention?
An omission happens when important information from the source is left out. An invention happens when the AI adds information that was not present or supported by the source.
3. Why must numerical information be checked separately?
Numbers can easily be changed, misplaced or misunderstood by AI. Checking them separately helps ensure that the summary accurately represents the original data.
4. Why should source limitations be included in a summary?
Source limitations show what the report does not establish. Including them prevents readers from assuming that unavailable information has been measured or confirmed.
5. What is a report scaffold?
A report scaffold is a structured framework containing the main sections and headings of a report. It helps organise information before the final factual content is completed.
6. Why should report structure be separated from factual content?
Separating structure from facts prevents headings and planned sections from being mistaken for verified information. It also makes it easier to identify which information still needs evidence.
7. What is the purpose of a [VERIFY] placeholder?
A [VERIFY] placeholder identifies information that must be checked or confirmed before the report is finalised. It helps prevent AI-generated assumptions from being treated as facts.
8. Can a concise summary still be misleading? Explain.
Yes. A summary can be short and readable but still misleading if it removes important limitations, changes figures or adds unsupported conclusions. Conciseness should not come at the cost of accuracy.
9. How did peer review improve your summary?
Peer review provided another check on the accuracy, clarity and completeness of the summary. It helped confirm that the figures and limitations were presented correctly.
10. Why should an AI-generated management report be reviewed before use?
AI can misunderstand information, omit important details or generate unsupported claims. A human reviewer should verify the facts, figures, context and recommendations before the report is used for management decisions.
